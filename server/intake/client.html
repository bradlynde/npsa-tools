    Your identity

    What you believe and stand for, and why people know you.

    Name the theological, social, or political stances you hold publicly, especially any that have drawn criticism, protest, or outside attention: an affirming stance on inclusion, a pro-life or pro-choice position, support for Israel or for Palestinian rights, refugee or sanctuary work, traditional teaching on marriage, racial justice advocacy. Organizations across the whole spectrum receive NSGP awards. Be specific and honest; the risk narrative is built on this answer. Include a link to your statement of faith, beliefs, or values.
    Think about your building, visible symbols, history, reputation, or location (a busy intersection, downtown).
    For example: the largest church in the county, or the only Jewish day school in the region.
    Well-known members, national media attention, or a leading voice on certain issues.

    For example: about 400 children under 12 on Sundays, 85 seniors at a weekly lunch, 20 to 30 veterans at a monthly support group, 40 to 60 people experiencing homelessness at a Saturday meal.

    Working with first responders
    A walkthrough, assessment, training, or incident response. Tell us the agency, what they did, and roughly when. For example: March 2024, two Frisco PD officers walked the campus and recommended door upgrades.
    County emergency management forums, faith-based security coalitions, CISA briefings, sheriff outreach, InfraGard. Say what and how often.

    During an incident, an investigation, or as a standing arrangement. What, with whom, and when.

    Section 4 of 5

    The threats you face

    Incidents and risks at and around your facility.

    Include anything, even if it seems minor; we decide what is relevant. For example: 12/2024, someone pulled open a locked door after hours, report #24-12345. 3/2023, swastika on a rear wall, report #23-67890.
    Threats, vandalism, break-ins, or attacks you know about firsthand, not news stories (our research team covers those). What, where, and roughly when.
    Counter-protesters, media criticism, or organized opposition. For example: a pro-life speaker in 2023 drew heavy social media backlash and needed police coordination.
    Section 5 of 5

    The project

    Past grants, current systems, and who will manage the grant.

    If yes, the year and amount.
    Can police view your cameras remotely? Do they have keys or access codes? Does a monitoring service call police? For example: ADT monitors our alarm and dispatches police; PD pulls footage on request.
    Two or three people covering Project Manager (timeline, procurement, reporting), Budget Manager (spending, financial reporting), and Facilities Manager (vendors, installation). One person can hold more than one role. For each: name, title (employer if a volunteer), phone, email, and two or three sentences on relevant experience.
